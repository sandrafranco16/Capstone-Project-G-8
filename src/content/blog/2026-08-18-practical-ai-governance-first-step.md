AI governance can sound like a large policy project. For many organisations, the most
useful first step is much smaller: build a clear view of how AI is already being used.

## Create a simple AI use register

Ask teams to record the AI tools and workflows they currently use. Each entry should
capture:

- the business purpose;
- the person responsible for the workflow;
- the information provided to the tool;
- the people affected by its output; and
- the current review or approval process.

The register does not need to be a complex system. A carefully managed spreadsheet can
be enough for an initial review.

## Prioritise by impact

Not every use case needs the same level of oversight. Drafting an internal agenda is
different from screening job applicants or preparing advice for a customer. Review the
higher-impact uses first, especially those involving personal information or important
decisions.

## Turn findings into action

The first review should produce a short list of actions: stop an unsafe use, add a human
check, clarify an owner or test whether the workflow delivers a real benefit. This makes
governance part of everyday work rather than a document that sits apart from it.

Visibility creates the evidence needed for proportionate policies, training and
executive oversight.
