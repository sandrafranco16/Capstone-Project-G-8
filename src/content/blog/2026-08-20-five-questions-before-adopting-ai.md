AI adoption works best when it begins with a real problem rather than a new tool.
Before starting a pilot, ask five simple questions.

## 1. What problem are we solving?

Describe the current task, who performs it and why it matters. A specific problem such
as reducing manual report preparation is easier to evaluate than a broad goal such as
“using more AI”.

## 2. What would a useful result look like?

Choose a measurable outcome. It might be time saved, fewer repeated steps or a more
consistent first draft. The measure does not need to be complicated, but it should be
clear before the pilot begins.

## 3. What information will the tool receive?

Identify whether the workflow includes personal, confidential or commercially
sensitive information. Use approved tools and remove unnecessary data before testing.

## 4. Who checks the output?

AI output should have a named human reviewer. The level of review should increase when
the decision has a greater impact on customers, employees or the organisation.

## 5. When will we stop or change the pilot?

Set a review date and agree on the evidence needed to continue. A small, time-boxed
experiment creates useful learning without turning an untested idea into a permanent
process.
